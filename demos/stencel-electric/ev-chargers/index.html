Why everyone upgrades to Level 2

The charger that came in your trunk plugs into a wall outlet and trickles. A dedicated 240-volt circuit changes the math entirely.

Home charging — the honest comparison
L1

Level 1 — the wall outlet

Standard 120V outlet, the cord from the trunk. Roughly 3–5 miles of range per hour. An overnight charge barely covers a commute, and a shared garage circuit can trip all night.

L2

Level 2 — the dedicated circuit

240V on its own breaker, hardwired or NEMA 14-50 outlet. Roughly 20–40+ miles of range per hour depending on your car and amperage — empty to full while you sleep, every night.

What actually sets the price

No "starting at $399" games. Four things decide what an EV charger install costs, and Justin walks you through each one before any work starts.

Your panel's capacity

A Level 2 charger wants a 40–60 amp circuit. Most modern panels have room; older or full panels may need a load calculation, a tandem rearrange, or an upgrade — known before you commit, not discovered mid-job.

Distance from panel to parking

Wire run is the biggest cost variable. Charger on the wall next to the panel is the easy case; across the house or out to a detached garage means more copper, conduit, and labor.

Hardwired vs. plug-in

A NEMA 14-50 outlet keeps things flexible; hardwiring is cleaner, often supports higher amperage, and is required by some chargers. Justin will tell you which your charger and usage actually call for.

Permit & inspection

EV circuits should be permitted and inspected — it protects your home insurance and resale. Stencel handles the paperwork and meets the inspector so you don't have to.

EV charger questions, answered straight

Can you install a Tesla Wall Connector?

Yes — Tesla Wall Connectors, Universal Wall Connectors, and every major third-party brand (ChargePoint, Emporia, JuiceBox, Grizzl-E, and more). If you haven't bought one yet, Justin will recommend the right unit for your car and panel instead of the most expensive one.

Will my electrical panel need an upgrade?

Often not — many homes have the capacity for a 40–50 amp circuit. When a panel is genuinely full or undersized, you'll know up front with a load calculation, and you'll get options at more than one price point.

How long does the installation take?

A typical garage install with reasonable wire run is a half-day. Long runs, detached garages, or panel work can extend that — your quote will say so before anything starts.

Do you do commercial or fleet charging?

Yes. Justin is a commercial electrician by trade — workplace charging, customer-facing stations, and multi-unit setups are all in scope. Commercial jobs get a site visit and a scoped proposal.

Is a permit really necessary?

A new 240V circuit should be permitted and inspected — it's what keeps your insurance clean and your home sale smooth later. Stencel pulls the permit and handles the inspection as part of the job, not as an extra.
